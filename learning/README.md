# Learning route

The single educational anchor is **Daniel Gottesman, _An Introduction to Quantum Error Correction and Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation_, arXiv:0904.2557v1 (16 April 2009)**. Use the [versioned source](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2557v1) and the [Gottesman-to-project bridge](GOTTESMAN_BRIDGE.md). Section numbers below refer to that version, not to the shorter earlier chapter.

The [30-milestone understanding map](MILESTONES.md) has **12 background/preliminaries milestones** and **18 narrative/innovation milestones**. The first phase connects qubit states, Pauli errors, stabilizer operations, acceptance and error conventions into the factory's complete contract. The second follows the converse and construction through to the joint raw-copy law and its limitations. This allocation gives the new kernel mechanism and its operational consequence more space than the prerequisites, without treating every technical lemma as a separate lesson.

Start with the bridge's worked example, then use the milestone map alongside the [scientific reading route](../science/README.md). A reader familiar with stabilizer codes can go directly to the bridge's notation table and the [model](../science/model.md). The milestone targets and dependencies are common to all readers; this directory contains no personal assessments, retry rules or learner history.

Gottesman supplies the coding and fault-tolerance foundations. The bridge supplies project-specific definitions, conditional-output bookkeeping and the route from universal exact conversion to a rejection kernel. The algebraic-geometry correcting code, exact concentrator and small detector remain separately credited research ingredients in [related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md), not additional educational anchors.

For each claim, the authoritative statement and proof remain under [science/](../science/README.md). For computational evidence, use the [verification guide](../verification/README.md); a finite check is not a replacement for a universal proof.
